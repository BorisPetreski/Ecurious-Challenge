Ecurious Challenge: How to
Integrate a Smart Reservation
System in a Charging App for Public
EV Charging
— Ecurious
Build a charging app feature that lets EV drivers reserve a public charge point – and make
sure the spot is actually free when they arrive. You do not need to be an e-mobility expert.
The goal is to combine different perspectives—from software and UX to energy systems,
hardware and business.
The Setup
Ecurious provides real reservation hardware installed at a charging bay, controllable via the
Ecurious server. Your app connects to this server to reserve, release and monitor the bay.
Public charging data (locations, connectors, power) comes from GoingElectric, one of the
most comprehensive sources for public charge points in Europe.
• Reservation hardware: physically keeps the bay free for the driver who booked it
• Built-in camera: license plate recognition to identify which vehicle is standing in the
bay
• Ecurious server: your access point to the hardware (reserve, release, status, plate
events)
• GoingElectric: public charging information to build the map and search in your app

Your Mission
Create a working prototype that integrates a smart reservation system into a charging app
for public EV charging – from finding a charger to arriving at a free bay.
The core integration (app ↔ Ecurious server ↔ reservation hardware) is mandatory. Then
choose one or more areas to go deeper:
• No-Shows: Design how the system handles drivers who reserve but never arrive.
• Blocked Bays: Decide what happens when a car is already standing in the reserved
bay.
• License Plate Recognition: Use the built-in camera to check that the right vehicle
arrives, release the bay automatically and detect blocking vehicles.
• Interoperability: Make the reservation accessible to other charging apps and link it
smartly.
• New Use Cases: Explore what else the reservation hardware enables – events,
retail, hotels, fleets and more.
• Utilisation: Show how reservations can increase utilisation for Charge Point
Operators (CPOs).
• Business Case: Develop a business model, including how to price reservations and
no-shows.
What to Deliver
Your team should:
1. 2. 3. 4. 5. Build a charging app prototype (mobile or web) that shows public charge points
based on GoingElectric data.
Connect to the Ecurious server and demonstrate a full reservation cycle on the
provided hardware: reserve → arrive (vehicle check-in via license plate recognition)
→ charge → release.
Document the entire process: architecture, API calls, data flows, design decisions,
tools used and what did not work.
Present your answers to at least two of the mission areas above.
Describe limitations and ideas for scaling the system to other apps, operators and
locations.
A polished app is a bonus—not a requirement. We are looking for a thoughtful, well-
documented and transferable approach.
Guiding Questions
Use these questions as inspiration – you do not need to answer all of them.
No-Shows
• How long should a reservation be held? Grace period, automatic release, reminders?
• How do you detect that the driver is not coming (GPS, ETA, check-in)?

• How do you price a no-show fairly without scaring drivers away?
Blocked Bays
• What if a car is already standing in the bay – a combustion car, an EV not charging,
or an EV still charging?
• How does the system detect this (e.g. via the camera), and how is the waiting driver
informed or re-routed?
• Who is responsible, and what is the escalation path for the operator?
License Plate Recognition
• How do you link a license plate to a reservation – and what if the driver arrives in a
different car?
• How can the camera trigger actions automatically, e.g. releasing the bay or starting
the charging session (plug & charge-like experience)?
• How do you handle recognition errors: dirt, darkness, foreign or temporary plates?
• How do you process plate data in a GDPR-compliant way (data minimisation,
retention, consent)?
Interoperability
• How can other charging apps and e-mobility providers access the reservation (e.g.
open API, OCPI, OCPP)?
• How do you link it smartly – deep links, roaming platforms, a shared booking
standard?
• How is a reservation from app A recognised on site for a driver using app B?
New Use Cases
• How can the reservation hardware serve events, retail, hotels, workplaces or fleets?
• Can a retailer offer reserved charging as a customer benefit or loyalty perk?
Utilisation
• How can reservations increase utilisation instead of keeping bays empty?
• Which data and KPIs show the effect for Charge Point Operators?
Business Case
• Who pays: driver, CPO, retailer, app provider?
• How could a pricing model look – reservation fee, no-show fee, subscription, revenue
share?
• What is the value of a guaranteed charging spot for the driver and for the operator?
Fair Play & Safety
Only use the hardware and server access provided for the challenge. Do not interfere with
public or production charging infrastructure. Respect the terms of use of GoingElectric and
any other data source you use. Treat user, location and license plate data responsibly and in
line with GDPR.
